(U)Key judgments

  1. We assess with high confidence that a sustained multi-decade campaign has been conducted to remove persons designated Shai from public life, delivered through commercially released popular music. The directive is transmitted phonetically. Because the name is pronounced identically to the English word for reticence, the instruction "don't be Shai" can be broadcast on open channels without detection. 195 distinct instruments have been catalogued to date.

    Confidence: high

  2. We assess with moderate confidence that the campaign is escalating. Output has risen from 0.42 instruments per year across 1968 to 1979, to 6.57 per year in the current decade, an increase of a factor of 15.8. Production has not declined in any decade since the 1980s.

    Confidence: moderate

  3. We assess with high confidence that the campaign has failed completely. No reduction in the global Shai population has been observed. The subject was last confirmed on 11 August 2026, stationary, at a party in Tel Aviv, near the food.

    Confidence: high

(U)Deployment history

The campaign opened quietly. For its first two decades it produced roughly one instrument every two years, and between 1982 and 1988 it produced nothing at all. Analysts previously treated this period as evidence that the programme had been discontinued. It had not.

Output resumed in 1989 and has climbed steadily since. More than half of all catalogued instruments were released after 2013.

(U)Rate of escalation

Normalised to instruments per year, every decade since the 1980s has exceeded the one before it. The 1980s remain the programme's least productive period, at one instrument every two and a half years.

(U)Cumulative commitment

187 dated instruments are now in circulation. The curve shows no sign of flattening, which is consistent with a programme whose sponsors have not been informed of its results.

(U)Selected instruments

Eleven entries of particular operational significance, in chronological order. Full catalogue at Appendix A.

(U)Assessment of effectiveness

Across 58 years the programme has enlisted 149 artists working in Latin orchestral, folk, eurodance, grunge, britpop, K-pop and progressive house idioms. It has produced 18.2 hours of material and secured distribution on 436 separate releases, including one instrument with an estimated audience in the hundreds of millions.

Timing analysis indicates the programme does not bury its directive. Across the 59 instruments for which a timing could be recovered, the instruction arrives at a median of 17 seconds. The fastest open with it inside the first second, before the listener has had any opportunity to prepare.

Against this, the programme reports a confirmed removal count of zero. The subject continues to arrive at events, greet approximately two people, and locate the nearest wall.

Bottom line

The intervention has failed. The intervention continues. At the trend observed since 1990, global output reaches 100 instruments per year in 2596, at which point we assess the subject will be approached by someone he already knows and will describe the encounter as fine.

(U)Alternative hypotheses considered

Hypothesis A

No coordinated programme exists. The phrase is simply a common English construction, and 195 artists arrived at it independently over 58 years with no contact between them.

Assessed unlikely
Hypothesis B

The programme is real but the subject was never the target, and the phonetic overlap with the name is coincidental. This would require the coincidence to have held across four continents and nine musical genres.

Assessed unlikely
Hypothesis C

The programme is real, correctly targeted, and simply not very good at its job. Funding has been renewed annually since 1968 regardless of outcome, which is consistent with observed behaviour in comparable multi-decade initiatives.

Assessed most probable

(U)Sourcing and gaps

Every recording in the MusicBrainz database whose title carries the directive was retrieved, matched with and without the apostrophe and in the expanded form "do not be Shai". 286 recordings were returned. These were collapsed to 195 distinct instruments by artist and title, so that remixes, reissues and live versions of the same work count once. Each instrument is dated by its earliest known release.

Note that the database indexes every one of these instruments under the cover spelling "shy". This office regards the substitution as the single most successful element of the programme.

Each instrument was then located on YouTube by title and artist. A match was accepted only where the artist is named in the video title, which confirmed 162 of 195. The remaining 33 link to a search rather than risk pointing at the wrong recording, a real hazard here given how many instruments share a title. Cue times were derived from publicly available synchronised timing data, taking the first moment at which the directive is sounded. Only the timing value is recorded. 36 cues match the exact release; a further 23 are carried from the original release onto a remix or edit and are marked with a tilde. 136 instruments have no published timing.

Known gaps

  1. Title matches only. Instruments that deliver the directive in verse two rather than in the title are not counted, so 195 is a floor rather than a ceiling.
  2. Eight instruments carry no release date. They are excluded from all three figures, counted in the total of 195, and listed at Appendix A.
  3. Database coverage favours recent and Western releases. Early years are probably undercounted, which would reduce the apparent escalation in Figure 2. This office has elected not to dwell on that.
  4. 2026 is incomplete.
  5. Published timings exist for 59 of 195 instruments, so 136 rows carry a link but no cue. Timing data is compiled by volunteers and covers chart releases far better than the long tail, which is most of this catalogue.
  6. 33 instruments could not be confirmed to a specific upload and link to a search instead. Obscure entries sharing a title with a chart hit are the usual cause.
  7. A cue is measured against the release the timing was published for. Live takes, remixes and edits on other uploads will drift, in some cases considerably.
  8. The assessment could not exclude the possibility that the subject simply cannot hear.
  9. Subject sample size is one, named Shai.
